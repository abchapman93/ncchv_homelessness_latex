\subsection{Assumptions}
\subsubsection{Causal identification}

We make the following standard causal inference assumptions.

\paragraph{Conditional exchangeability.}  First, we assume that, conditional on baseline covariates and baseline housing status, potential outcomes are independent of treatment initiation during the grace period:
\begin{align*}
    Y^{\bar{1}_s}(T_A + 120), Y^{\bar{0}}(T_A + 120) \perp \Delta_A \mid H(0), \boldsymbol{L}, \boldsymbol{X}(0) \quad \text{for all } s \in [0, 30]
\end{align*}



\paragraph{Consistency.}
\begin{align*}
    Y^{\bar{1}_{T_A}}(t) &= Y(t) \quad \text{if } \Delta_A = 1
\end{align*}

\paragraph{Positivity.}
\begin{align*}
    0 < P(\Delta_A=1 \mid H(0), \boldsymbol{L}, \boldsymbol{X}(0)) < 1 \quad \text{for all } H(0), \boldsymbol{L}, \boldsymbol{X}(0)
\end{align*}


\subsubsection{Observed housing status}
Housing status may be fully or partially observed at time $t$ depending on the patient's vital status, program enrollment status, and healthcare visits at time $t$. By fully observed we mean that we can determine the exact value of $H(t)$. By partially observed, we mean that we have information about $H(t)$ from manifest variables but cannot determine the exact value. By not observed, we mean that we cannot directly determine anything about $H(t)$.

\paragraph{Direct observations of housing.} We assume that housing status is fully observed in four settings. First, for all patients at $t=0$ (time of Call Center contact) through the Center's assessment. Second, at the time of death, since $D(t)=1$ implies $Y(t) = 0$. Third, when a patient is enrolled in GPD ($X_3(t)=1$), since GPD enrollment implies $H(t) = 0$. Fourth, when a patient enters SSVF ($t = T_A$).

\paragraph{Partial observations of housing.}
Housing status is partially observed for patients living in HUD-VASH housing ($X_2(t)=1$), which implies $H(t) = 1$.

For patients enrolled in HUD-VASH who are in the process of obtaining HUD-VASH housing ($X_1(t)=1$, $X_2(t)=0$), we know they are not yet stably housed ($H(t) = 0$).

For patients who enrolled in SSVF and exited SSVF prior to follow-up, housing status at exit $H(T^Z)$ is assumed observed.

Define indicators $H^{\text{obs}}(t)$ for whether housing status is observed at time $t$ and $Y^{\text{obs}}$ for whether the primary outcome $Y$ is observed at follow-up.
The observation process for $Y$ is less restrictive than for $H$ since we can determine $Y=0$ if we know the patient is deceased or homeless, even if we don't have direct observation of housing.

\subsection{Follow-up strata}

Partition patients into strata defined by their vital status and program enrollment at follow-up time $T_A + 120$. Let $C = C(\boldsymbol{L}, \overline{\boldsymbol{X}}(T_A + 120), D(T_A + 120))$ denote stratum membership:

\begin{itemize}
    \item $C = 0$: Deceased ($D(T_A + 120) = 1$)
    \item $C = 1$: Enrolled in GPD ($D(T_A + 120) = 0$, $X_3(T_A + 120) = 1$)
    \item $C = 2$: Enrolled in HUD-VASH but not living in HUD-VASH housing, not enrolled in GPD ($D(T_A + 120) = 0$, $X_1(T_A + 120) = 1$, $X_2(T_A + 120) = 0$, $X_3(T_A + 120) = 0$)
    \item $C = 3$: Living in HUD-VASH housing ($D(T_A + 120) = 0$, $X_2(T_A + 120) = 1$)
    \item $C = 4$: Not enrolled in any VA homeless program at $T_A + 120$, but enrolled in and exited a VA homeless program between $t = 0$ and $T_A + 120$
    \item $C = 5$: Never enrolled in any VA homeless program between $t = 0$ and $T_A + 120$
\end{itemize}

For $C \in \{0, 1, 2\}$, the primary outcome is determined directly: $Y = 0$. For $C \in \{3, 4, 5\}$, $Y$ must be estimated from manifest variables via the latent variable model.
